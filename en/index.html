Why this matters

The image is changing. Transparency must keep up.

AI has permanently changed the film landscape. It is increasingly difficult to tell whether image, sound or text was partly or fully generated. Origin Report gives makers a simple way to be clear about it.

01 / TRUST

A declaration, not a label

The makers record what happened. Origin Report does not verify or certify, but provides a clear, comparable way to declare AI use.

02 / NUANCE

Not all use is equal

Two altered shots are not the same as a fully generated film. That is why the production process is divided into eleven recognisable stages.

03 / OPENNESS

Take concerns seriously

Concerns about authorship, labour and provenance are understandable. Concrete transparency is more useful than a generic label.

Made on the floor

Frequently asked questions

Openness starts with clear answers.

Why is Origin Report free?

Transparency only works when the barrier is low. Makers should be able to explain their use of AI without a subscription, account or dependency on a vendor. The format and basic declaration therefore remain freely accessible.

Why should I use it?

It is not mandatory and it is not a certification. An Origin Report does show that you take responsibility for your process. It gives audiences, collaborators, funders and clients a concrete, nuanced answer instead of a generic statement.

What can I do with a report?

You can include the short declaration in credits or a press kit, send it with festival submissions and use it when broadcasters, funds, distributors or clients ask about AI. The full file can be stored or published alongside the production.

Why is it a JSON file?

JSON is a small, open text format that can be read by people and computers. That makes the report portable and allows the same information to be reused later in festival forms, databases or a public registry without entering it again.

What does IPTC mean?

IPTC is an international organisation that maintains standards for information attached to news and media files. Where relevant, Origin Report references its existing terminology for digital media provenance. You do not need to know IPTC to use the form.

Can’t a maker simply lie?

Yes. Origin Report is a declaration, not technical detection or independent verification. Someone who knowingly makes a false declaration mainly damages their own credibility and may also breach agreements with festivals or clients. The system is based on responsibility, signature and transparency.

When will there be a public registry?

When makers and organisations prove that the format is useful and there is enough demand for searching and comparison. First, the declaration needs to show that it is clear, practical and complete in real productions.
