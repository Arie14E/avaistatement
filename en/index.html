Why this matters

Transparency matters.

AI has permanently changed the film landscape. It is increasingly difficult to tell whether image, sound or text was partly or fully generated. Audiovisueel AI Statement gives makers a simple way to be clear about it.

01 / TRUST

A declaration, not a label

The makers record what happened. Audiovisueel AI Statement does not verify or certify, but provides a clear, comparable way to declare AI use. Trust matters.

02 / NUANCE

Not all use is equal

Two altered shots are not the same as a fully generated film. That is why the production process is divided into thirteen recognisable stages.

03 / OPENNESS

Take concerns seriously

Concerns about authorship, labour and provenance are understandable. Concrete transparency is more useful than a generic label.

Why now

The question no longer comes from audiences alone.

In little more than a year, transparency about AI shifted from a moral question to something legislators, commissioners and unions ask for. Four developments behind this format.

2 August 2026

The EU obligation now applies

Article 50 of the EU AI Act has applied since this date. The duty sits with the deployer, meaning the producer and not the tool vendor. Fiction gets a lighter variant: disclose that generated material exists, in a way that does not hamper enjoyment of the work. What that means in practice has been left open.

June 2026

Broadcasters ask for a fitting approach

EBU, ACT, VAUNET and five other bodies jointly called on the European Commission to refrain from blanket labelling duties. Their concern: if warnings appear everywhere, nobody notices them. They ask for an approach that matches how films are actually made.

2023 tot 2026

Commissioners demand documentation

Netflix requires prior written approval for digital replicas of real people. SAG-AFTRA requires separate, signed consent per project. The US Copyright Office requires AI material to be excluded at registration; concealing it can invalidate the registration.

September 2025

Music got there first

The DDEX standard was adopted by Spotify and rolled out through distributors. The decisive detail: not a single “contains AI” tick, but separate fields for vocals, instrumentation, composition, mixing and lyrics. That is why platforms could act on it.

Frequently asked questions

Openness starts with clear answers.

Why is Audiovisueel AI Statement free?

Transparency only works when the barrier is low. Makers should be able to explain their use of AI without a subscription, account or dependency on a vendor. Creating, publishing and reading therefore stays free, and whatever is public is fully visible to everyone. There will be no richer version for those who pay.

Why should I use it?

It is not mandatory and it is not a certification. An Audiovisueel AI Statement does show that you take responsibility for your process. It gives audiences, collaborators, funders and clients a concrete, nuanced answer instead of a generic statement.

What can I do with a report?

You can include the short declaration in credits or a press kit, send it with festival submissions and use it when broadcasters, funds, distributors or clients ask about AI. The full file can be stored or published alongside the production. Whoever receives it gets the PDF you downloaded: a readable document anyone can open, even without this site.

Why is it a JSON file?

JSON is a small, open text format that can be read by people and computers. That makes the report portable and allows the same information to be reused later in festival forms, databases or a public registry without entering it again.

What does IPTC mean?

IPTC is an international organisation that maintains standards for information attached to news and media files. Where relevant, Audiovisueel AI Statement references its existing terminology for digital media provenance. You do not need to know IPTC to use the form.

Can’t a maker simply lie?

Yes. Audiovisueel AI Statement is a declaration, not technical detection or independent verification. Someone who knowingly makes a false declaration mainly damages their own credibility and may also breach agreements with festivals or clients. The system is based on responsibility, signature and transparency.

When will there be a public registry?

When makers and organisations prove that the format is useful and there is enough demand for searching and comparison. First, the declaration needs to show that it is clear, practical and complete in real productions.
